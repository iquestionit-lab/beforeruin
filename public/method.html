How This Work Is Done
Method  ·  Tools  ·  What You Can Do Yourself Come And Do Likewise BR-MT-001  · 

A generation ago this took a library card, a reading-room chair, and years. That is no longer true — and the change is worth handing over rather than hiding.

Abstract

This page is written for the person sitting in a Bible study who wants to check something and does not know where to start. It sets out how the work on this site is actually done: what the tools are, what they are genuinely good for, what they cannot do at all, and where a person has to stand.

The short version. I use AI for the footwork — finding, retrieving, cross-checking, keeping track. It is very good at that, and it costs almost nothing, where forty years ago the same work meant travel, fees, and most of a decade. What it does not do is ask, judge, or answer for what is published. Nothing reaches you that I have not gone back to the source and read for myself. Where I could not reach a source, the page says so. Where a conclusion is mine rather than the field's, it is marked as a reading. Where the evidence cuts against me, that is printed too.

And there is one more thing, which turns out to be the whole point: using these tools well is a skill, and it is not the same skill as operating them. That part is teachable, so most of this page is spent teaching it.

First

The tool did not ask the question

Everything downstream depends on where the question came from.

No machine woke up one morning and wondered whether the Holy Ghost was a ghost. None of them sat in a cold room as a boy and noticed that the household had only one voice in it. Nothing in a data centre has ever been handed a God described as Father and then looked around the room for the mother.

The question came from a life. The tool only ever answered what it was asked.

That is not a small distinction, and it is not a defensive one. In research, the question is most of the work. Anyone who has done it will tell you the same: a well-formed question aimed at the right place will find things a thousand hours of undirected reading never turns up. A badly formed one returns a tidy answer to nothing.

Every question on this site came from a man who had been troubled by something for years before any of these tools existed. What changed is how long it took to go and look.

Second

What the tools do, and what they do not

Worth being plain about both, because the line between them is where the work lives.

The footwork — genuinely good at this

  • Finding which scholars have written on a question, and which of them the field actually reads
  • Retrieving a passage in Syriac, Hebrew or Greek and laying the versions side by side
  • Cross-checking a claim against several sources at once and flagging where they part company
  • Keeping track of hundreds of citations without losing one
  • Turning a half-remembered line into a chapter and a page number
  • Doing all of it in an evening rather than over a term

What no tool does

  • Ask a question that came out of somebody's life
  • Know which answer matters and which is merely correct
  • Notice when a source is confidently wrong
  • Feel the pull to accept the reading that helps, and refuse it
  • Decide what a congregation is ready to hear, and how to say it without wounding anyone
  • Stand behind it afterward

I spent forty years in a trade where nobody hand-calculates a service load. You use the tables. You use the calculator. Nobody thinks the less of you for it, and the work is better for it. But an apprentice does not get the tables on day one. He gets them after he has done enough by hand to know when a number looks wrong — because the table will happily give you an answer to a question you asked incorrectly, and the table is not the one who has to sign the permit.

Third

The part that is a skill

Anyone can type a question. Knowing what came back is a different matter.

These tools do not fail loudly. They fail plausibly — a citation with the right shape and a wrong page number, a title that has drifted one word, a summary that is accurate about a book nobody in the field takes seriously any more. None of that announces itself. It arrives looking exactly like everything else.

So the skill is not operating the thing. The skill is knowing what an answer should look like before you get one, and noticing when the shape is off.

A live example from this site

While building The Witnesses, a chapter by Sebastian Brock came back under two different titles. One said Feminine. One said Mother. Only one is on the book's contents page.

Notice which way it drifted. Mother is the reading that helps my argument. It is still the one I set down — because it is not what the contents page says, and a citation that flatters the person citing it is exactly the one to check twice.

No tool caught that. No tool could. It was caught because I knew what the citation ought to look like, and that one looked a little too good.

That is the nuance, and it is why this is not a button. It comes from years of reading, from having been wrong in public, and from wanting the truth more than you want the argument. It can be learned. It is learned the same way anything is learned in a trade: by doing it, being corrected, and doing it again.

Fourth

How to do this yourself

You can check anything on this site tonight, from a kitchen table, for nothing.

Start with something that has bothered you.

Not a topic — a snag. A verse that never sat right, a word your pastor used oddly, a thing you were told that you could never quite square. The snag is the question, and the question is most of the work.

Find the word underneath the word.

Open an interlinear or a lexicon and see what stands under the English. Logeion gives you the standard Greek and Latin dictionaries free. Blue Letter Bible and STEP Bible will show you the Hebrew and Greek behind any verse without costing a cent.

Ask who has written on it — then go get the actual book.

This is where a tool earns its keep: it will tell you in a minute who the field reads on your question. Then stop using it and get the book. Interlibrary loan is free at most public libraries. Archive.org lends. Many scholars post their own work.

Look at the manuscript yourself.

You do not need permission and you do not need Greek. Codex Sinaiticus is online page by page. The Dead Sea Scrolls are photographed at full resolution. Look at the thing itself at least once — it changes how you read everything after.

Go looking for who disagrees.

Deliberately. Before you are attached to the answer. If you cannot state the best case against your position in its own strongest form, you do not understand your position yet — you only like it.

Write down which part is yours.

Somewhere in every piece of work there is a seam between what the sources say and what you concluded from them. Find that seam and mark it. On this site those are tagged Reading. It costs nothing and it is the difference between teaching and selling.

Conclusion

The footwork is cheap now. The judgement never was.

Which is good news, and it is the reason this page exists.

What used to guard this work was cost. Thousands of dollars, years of access, a chair in a room most people would never be admitted to. That gate is largely gone, and I am not sorry to see it go. A man in Auburn, New York can now read the same manuscripts as a man in Oxford, on the same evening, for nothing.

There was a second gate, and it is the one this site is named against. What guarded the meaning was not cost but difficulty — the settled impression that the thing said was complicated, and that you would need somebody to explain it to you before you could have it. It was not complicated when it was said. It became complicated in the handling: in the translation committee, in the doctrine hardening around it, in every edit made by someone who needed it to read a particular way. What is on this site is the reading before that handling, with the handling shown.

What is left is the part that was always the real work — asking a question worth asking, recognising a bad answer, resisting the reading that flatters you, and standing behind what you publish. None of that was ever for sale, and none of it has been automated.

The tools did the fetching. The asking, the weighing, and the answering for it are mine.

If you want to check any of it, everything you need is listed on The Witnesses and on each episode's page. Most of it is free. I would rather you go and look than take my word for it — that is the whole posture of this work, and it applies to the method as much as to the conclusions.

Where I fall short, and what I do about it

I am one man, working evenings, caring for my mother, without a department behind me. There are sources I have not been able to reach and languages I read only with help. When that is the case, the page says so in plain words rather than smoothing it over.

If you find something here that is wrong, I would genuinely rather know than not. Corrections go straight onto the page with the date.
