⬥ Featured Definition — Current Edition

Faith

n.  /  πίστις (pistis)  ·  אֱמוּנָה (emunah)

Not a feeling. Not optimism. Not belief without evidence. The question is not whether you trust — it is whether your trust moves you.

Faith, in the tradition from which it is drawn, was never a synonym for hope or sentiment. The Greek πίστις and the Hebrew אֱמוּנָה converge on one operational idea: reliable action based on demonstrated trustworthiness. A bridge engineer who walks across his own structure is exercising faith. A man who claims to trust the bridge but will not cross it is exercising nothing.

Creation bears witness. The physical world was organized to point toward its Maker.

Believe and Belief are not the same word.

Verb vs. noun. The verb demands action. The noun describes a structure. James 2 depends entirely on this distinction.

⬩ Connective Tissue — Below

The small words carry the weight. By, through, in, from — one preposition misread changes who holds first cause.

Connective Tissue Small Words. Enormous Consequences.

English translations collapse dozens of distinct Greek prepositions into a handful of common words — by, through, in, from, of. Each original word carries a specific relational direction. When these distinctions vanish, verses that name God as first cause become verses that name Jesus — and verses about the origin of faith point to human effort rather than divine initiation.
