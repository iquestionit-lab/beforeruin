How To Ask
Passage  ·  Term  ·  Question The Question Is Most Of The Work BR-SR-001  · 

Most people do not fail to find the answer. They never get a clean question out of their mouth — and the tool answers the question it was actually asked.

Abstract

This page teaches one skill: how to turn the thing that is bothering you into a question a search can answer. It sets out what each of the three doors returns, what to have ready before you type, how to read what comes back, and what to do when nothing does. It takes about two minutes and it will change what you get for the rest of your life.

Start with the honest position. You are sitting somewhere — a pew, a kitchen table, a study group — and a word has snagged on you. Someone read it aloud and it did not sit right. You do not know Greek. You do not know Hebrew. You are not going to learn them this afternoon.

Good. None of that is required. What is required is the English word that snagged, and a willingness to find out it was carrying something else.

You do not need the original language. You need to notice that the English is doing too much work.
Content · One
The three doors
Each one answers a different shape of not-knowing.

Passage — you know the address

You have a reference. Romans 10:17. John 1:3. Type it and you get that verse opened up: the original words underneath the English, with the places translation moved something marked where they happen. Use this when you want to see a verse you already know in the language it was written in.

Term — you know the word, and you suspect it has moved

Faith. Repent. Hate. Believe. One word, by itself. You get what it carried before the drift, and the passages where the drift changes the reading. Use this when a word keeps turning up and you are no longer sure it means what you were told it means.

Question — you do not have the word yet

Who created the world? What does "hate your father and mother" mean? Ask it the way you would ask a person standing next to you. It finds the passage and decodes it for you. Use this one most. It is the door for the feeling that came before the vocabulary.

If you are not sure which door, use Question. It is the forgiving one. It will find the passage and hand you the terms, and then you can go back and look those up properly.

Content · Two
Five things to bring with you
These are what people leave behind, and what leaving them behind costs.

Bring the word that bothered you, not your summary of it

"Something about faith and works" returns something about faith and works. "Faith without works is dead" returns the verse, the Greek behind dead, and why James and Paul are not fighting. Quote the snag. Do not paraphrase it.

Bring one thing at a time

Two questions in one line produces an answer that half-serves both. Ask about hate. Then ask about father and mother. Two clean answers beat one blended one, every time.

Bring the context, in four words

Spirit alone is a large field. Spirit of the LORD Judges is a specific one. You do not need a full sentence — you need enough to point.

Bring the wording you actually heard

Translations differ, and the difference is often the whole point. If you heard charity and the search returns love, you have already learned something. Type what was said, not what you think it should have said.

Bring a second search

The first answer is where the work starts. It will hand you a word you did not have before — a Greek term, a rare noun, a preposition doing more than its size suggests. Take that word and search it. That second search is where people find the thing they came for.

Content · Three
Watch one go
A real search, start to finish.

The snag. Someone says Samson's strength was in his hair. You have heard it your whole life and you have started to wonder whether the text ever said it.

The first search. You do not know the Hebrew, so you use Question: Samson's strength. Back comes the passage — and the line that the Spirit of the LORD came upon him, which is doing the work the hair gets credit for.

The second search. Now you have a phrase you did not have four seconds ago. You put Spirit of the LORD into Term. And now you are reading about something that runs from Judges to the Gospels, and the hair has become a detail in a story about somebody else's power.

Two searches. Under a minute. A thing you were taught wrong, corrected out of the text itself.
Content · Four
When nothing comes back
Usually the question, occasionally the record.

Widen by one step. A whole verse typed into Term returns little. Pull out the single word that carries the weight and ask again.

Change doors. If Term gives you nothing, the word may not be where the meaning lives. Put the sentence into Question and let it find the passage first.

Check the address. A reference that is off by a chapter returns an answer about the wrong thing, confidently. If what came back feels unrelated, look at the numbers before you doubt the tool.

And sometimes the answer is that nobody knows

There are words in the Hebrew Bible that appear once and are not securely understood by anyone. There are verses where the manuscripts disagree with each other. When you land on one of those, you have not failed — you have arrived at the edge of what survives, which is a real place and worth knowing about.

Where this happens, the page will say so. A search that tells you the ground is uncertain has told you something true.

Conclusion
The point of teaching you this
So you stop needing me.

A generation ago, checking a preacher meant a library card, a reading room, and a language you did not have. The honest result was that almost nobody checked, and a great deal was taught without ever being tested.

That is over. The whole apparatus now fits in the phone already in your hand in the pew. What has not changed is that somebody has to ask the question — and the tool cannot do that part for you.

So learn the question. Bring the word that snagged. Take the second search. And when someone tells you what a verse means, go and look. That is not disrespect. That is the highest compliment you can pay a teacher — that you took him seriously enough to check.
